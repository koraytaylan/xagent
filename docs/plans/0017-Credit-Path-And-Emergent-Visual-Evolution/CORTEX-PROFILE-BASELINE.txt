# Visual-Cortex Throughput Profile Baseline

## Measurement Parameters
- Test: `cortex_throughput_profile_baseline()` in `crates/xagent-sandbox/tests/integration.rs`
- Date: 2026-06-24
- Machine: Local Metal, M3 Max
- Frames: 100
- Retina resolution: 32×32
- Build: debug (unoptimized)
- Sub-component method: `BrainConfig::cortex_stage_limit` short-circuits
  `coop_visual_cortex` uniformly after each WGSL stage; incremental differences
  isolate per-stage wall-clock cost.

## Results at N=10 Agents

| Configuration                    | Throughput (tps) | Per-Frame Cost (μs) | vs Fused Baseline |
|----------------------------------|------------------|---------------------|-------------------|
| Cortex OFF (fused baseline)      | ~7,625           | 131.14              | —                 |
| Cortex ON — retina only          | ~8,693           | 115.03              | -12.28%           |
| Cortex ON — retina + DoG         | ~7,723           | 129.49              | -1.26%            |
| Cortex ON — all stages           | ~83              | 12,117.40           | +98.92%           |

### Sub-Component Breakdown (N=10, incremental stage cost)

| Sub-Component                          | Cost (μs) | % of Cortex Total |
|----------------------------------------|-----------|-------------------|
| Stage 0 — retina fill (luminance)      | ~15       | ~0.1%             |
| Stage 1 — DoG center-surround          | ~14       | ~0.1%             |
| Stages 2+3 — Gabor + quadrature + pool | ~11,988   | ~99.9%            |
| **Total cortex cost**                  | ~11,986   | 100%              |

**Dominant sub-component: Gabor bank + quadrature-energy + 4×4 MAX pooling**

## Summary

- **Measured cortex overhead (N=10)**: 98.92% of wall-clock time; cortex ON is
  ~93× slower than cortex OFF (~83 tps vs ~7,625 tps).
- **Measured cortex overhead (N=100)**: 99.04% of wall-clock time; cortex ON is
  ~103× slower than cortex OFF (~75 tps vs ~7,742 tps).
- The SCOPE.md spec states "~81 tps vs ~34,000 tps" (99.76% overhead, 420× slower).
  That figure came from a different measurement context (release build or different GPU
  configuration). The values here are the current measured baseline on this machine in
  a debug build; they are the canonical record for plan 0017 optimization comparisons.
- Both the N=10 and N=100 baselines are similar in absolute cortex cost (~12–13 ms),
  showing the cost is per-agent and roughly linear (100 agents × same single-workgroup
  dispatch; the cortex runs once per vision stride, not once per agent, but total GPU
  time still grows with population because the fused kernel dispatches one workgroup
  per agent — each agent's cortex runs in its own workgroup in parallel).

## Cortex Pipeline Description

The cortex implements dense Gabor convolutions over a 32×32 luminance retina:
1. Stage 0 — Retina fill: nearest-neighbor sampling from raycast buffer → 32×32
   luminance array. (~15 μs, ~0.1% of cortex cost)
2. Stage 1 — DoG center-surround: signed valid-region convolution of retina with
   zero-sum DoG kernel (radius ~3σ_surround). (~1–14 μs, ~0.0–0.1% of cortex cost)
3. Stages 2+3 — Gabor simple cells + quadrature-energy + MAX pooling:
   8 orientations × 2 scales × per-pixel even/odd Gabor convolutions on the DoG map,
   quadrature energy E = sqrt(even² + odd²), MAX-pooled over overlapping 4×4 cells
   → 128 complex-cell features. L2 normalization follows. (~12,000–13,250 μs, ~99.9%
   of cortex cost)

Total operations per-frame per-agent: ~32×32 × 2 (DoG) + 32×32 × 16 (Gabor) +
16k (quadrature) + 128 (pooling) ≈ 200k operations.

## Optimization Priorities (based on measured sub-component dominance)

1. **Gabor bank convolution** (critical — ~99.9% of cortex cost): The even/odd
   Gabor responses are recomputed analytically per-pixel per-filter, with no kernel
   caching. Reducing the number of filters (8→4 orientations) or the retina size
   (32×32→24×24) would directly cut this cost. Separable row/column factoring of
   the Gabor itself is the next step (separable-dog-optimization task; note: the
   separable optimization applies more directly to Gabor than to DoG based on this
   profile — DoG cost is negligible).
2. **Retina size reduction** (high impact): Reducing from 32×32 to 24×24 cuts
   input pixels by ~1.78×, directly reducing Gabor convolution work.
3. **Pooling radius reduction**: 4×4→3×3 saves ~1.2× per pool cell evaluation.
4. **DoG separable optimization** (negligible): DoG is ~0.1% of cortex cost.
   Implementing separable 1D DoG kernels would save <15 μs of a ~12,000 μs budget.
   Do not prioritize this over Gabor or retina-size work.

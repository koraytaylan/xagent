# Review: Agent Self-Awareness, Survival Drive, and Slow Food/Danger Learning

**Date:** 2026-06-12  
**Reviewer:** GPT-5 Codex  
**Scope:** current worktree review of the agent learning loop, including
`brain_passes.wgsl`, `kernel_tick.wgsl`, `phase_vision.wgsl`, `common.wgsl`,
`buffers.rs`, `config.rs`, `governor.rs`, and generation-state inheritance.

**Question under review:** after many iterations, agents still do not show
consistent danger avoidance or food chasing. Their behavior is not random, but
learning is not becoming visible fast enough. Is this an intelligence problem,
a bad definition of intelligence, or a missing survival instinct?

---

## Executive Summary

This is probably not an intelligence-capacity problem. It is a grounding,
observability, and temporal credit-assignment problem.

The system does contain a survival signal. Energy and integrity changes are
converted into a homeostatic gradient, urgency, and pain-amplified learning
signals in `coop_habituate_homeo()` and the predict-and-act credit path. What
the agents do not have is an innate survival policy. They are not told
"red terrain is danger" or "lime visual cluster is food." They must discover
that relationship from delayed correlations between sensory state, exploratory
motor noise, and later homeostatic change.

That is a coherent definition of minimal autonomous intelligence, but the
current implementation makes the required correlations hard to learn:

1. Sensory state and homeostatic consequence are not tightly aligned in time.
2. The encoder currently organizes around sensory variance, not survival value.
3. Memory valence can bind outcomes to the state/action present when the outcome
   arrives rather than the earlier action that caused it.
4. Hazard touch is not emitted by the live GPU sensory path, so danger has less
   direct sensory grounding than food.
5. The outer fitness function can reward competent random walking and survival
   without requiring clear within-lifetime food pursuit or danger avoidance.

The survival-instinct concern is valid, but in a precise sense: the agents have
homeostatic pressure, not enough built-in action structure or timely evidence to
turn that pressure into robust approach/avoid behavior on practical timescales.

---

## Finding 1: The Survival Signal Exists, but It Is Not a Survival Policy

`coop_habituate_homeo()` reads same-cycle normalized energy and integrity from
`physics_state`, computes deltas, combines them into a raw gradient, maintains
fast/medium/slow gradient EMAs, and scales the result by urgency. The relevant
constants are explicit in `common.wgsl`: energy and integrity weights,
multi-timescale blends, distress scale, credit decay, pain amplification, and
klinotaxis sensitivity.

That means "food good" and "danger bad" are available only as effects on
internal variables:

- Food becomes good because it produces a positive energy delta.
- Hazard becomes bad because it produces a negative integrity delta.
- Hunger becomes bad because low energy raises urgency and makes continued
  depletion more painful.

This is a defensible homeostatic definition of value. The key limitation is
that it provides scalar evaluation, not symbolic meaning. The system still has
to bind sensory cues and motor choices to later internal consequences.

**Interpretation:** the agents are not missing all survival drive. They are
missing fast, reliable cue-to-value binding.

---

## Finding 2: Sensory Evidence and Consequences Are Temporally Misaligned

The fused kernel order is:

```text
physics -> food_detect -> death_respawn -> brain
```

Within the same cycle, the brain reads current energy/integrity from
`physics_state`. However, the vision pass runs after the kernel dispatch, so
vision, touch, velocity, facing, angular state, and packed interoceptive
features in `sensory_buffer` come from the previous vision batch.

The kernel comments in `kernel_tick.wgsl` explicitly describe this split:

- `sensory_buffer` inputs are lagged by one batch.
- direct homeostasis reads from `physics_state` are same-cycle.

With default settings:

- `brain_tick_stride = 10`
- `vision_stride = 10`
- `tick_rate = 30 Hz`

the vision/touch/proprioceptive state can be stale relative to current
homeostatic consequence. Even if the exact lag varies by dispatch boundary, the
important learning fact is that the policy is often updating against a sensory
snapshot that is not the state that immediately caused the outcome.

For closed-loop behaviors like chasing food or steering out of danger, this is
especially costly. Pursuit requires rapid feedback: turn, observe bearing
change, correct. Avoidance requires the current hazard cue to line up with the
current damage signal.

**Interpretation:** before asking whether the agents are intelligent enough,
test whether they receive feedback at a timescale where intelligence can act.

## Finding 3: The Encoder Is Not Currently Value-Shaped

The current worktree's `brain_passes.wgsl` uses an Oja/PCA-style encoder update:

```wgsl
w = w + pca_lr * y * (x - y * w);
```

This is a reasonable fix for delayed-reward encoder corruption: it avoids using
a late reward event to train the encoder on the current, possibly irrelevant
visual frame. But it also changes the representation problem.

The encoder now preserves variance in sensory input. That does not guarantee it
preserves the features that matter most for survival. Terrain color, depth,
sky/background, velocity, and repeated visual structure may dominate variance,
while food and hazard may be sparse and behaviorally critical.

**Implication:** the action policy can become non-random because it is operating
over a structured encoded space, but that structure may not separate:

- food-left vs food-right,
- red danger ahead vs harmless terrain ahead,
- near-food state vs post-food state,
- turning away from danger vs turning while already safe.

**Interpretation:** this is not "no intelligence." It is unsupervised
representation learning that may not be aligned with survival relevance.

## Finding 4: Memory Valence Is Useful but Causally Loose

Memory recall blends stored motor outputs weighted by similarity and stored
valence:

- positive valence tends to reproduce a remembered action,
- negative valence tends to invert it.

This is a useful design. The weakness is timing. New memories store the current
encoded state, the current motor command, and the current raw homeostatic
gradient. If a reward or damage signal arrives after a multi-step trajectory,
the stored "good" or "bad" motor can be what the agent was doing when the
outcome arrived, not necessarily what caused the outcome.

The action history credit path attempts to solve this by storing exploratory
noise, prior gradient, and encoded state, then updating weights when the
gradient changes. That is directionally correct. But under delayed sensory
updates, persistent exploration noise, and sparse food/damage events, the
causal signal remains noisy.

**Interpretation:** the memory system can reinforce patterns, but it is not yet
a strong causal learner. It may remember "I felt good in a state like this"
without reliably learning "this earlier cue plus this turn caused food."

---

## Finding 5: Hazard Is Under-Grounded Compared With Food

The live GPU sensory path in `phase_vision_senses()` emits touch contacts for:

- food,
- other agents.

It does not currently emit `TOUCH_HAZARD` or `TOUCH_TERRAIN_EDGE`, even though
those tags exist in `common.wgsl` and the shared sensory contract reserves
them.

Food therefore has several grounding paths:

- lime visual cue from raycast,
- proximity touch contact,
- immediate positive energy jump on consumption.

Danger has fewer:

- reddish terrain/biome visual cue,
- negative integrity change after being in a danger biome,
- no live GPU hazard touch contact.

That makes hazard avoidance a harder association than food seeking. The agent
must infer that a visual terrain pattern predicts later integrity loss, often
through the same temporal lag described above.

**Interpretation:** inconsistent danger avoidance is expected until danger has
same-cycle or near-cycle sensory grounding.

---

## Finding 6: Evolution Inherits Learned State, but Fitness Does Not Force Clear Learning

The generation transition does preserve learned brain state. Champion slots get
the exact inherited `AgentBrainState`, and mutants receive perturbed encoder,
action, and predictor weights via `mutate_brain_state()`. So the current
failure mode is not simply "agents start from scratch every generation."

However, the governor fitness is:

```text
0.4 * survival + 0.3 * foraging + 0.3 * exploration
```

This can reward agents that survive, move, cover cells, and sometimes stumble
into food. It does not directly require the visible behavior the user is
looking for: "when food appears, turn toward it" and "when danger is sensed,
avoid it."

The initial forward bias, exploration noise, fatigue/staleness handling, and
klinotaxis layer can already produce non-random exploratory behavior. Evolution
can select parameters and inherited weights that make this random-walk strategy
less bad without producing crisp food pursuit or hazard avoidance.

**Interpretation:** the outer loop is selecting consequences, not necessarily
within-lifetime learning speed or cue-conditioned competence.

## Direct Answers

### Is this an intelligence problem?

Not primarily. The agents are structured, adaptive systems with predictive
state, memory, homeostasis, exploration, and inheritance. The observed weakness
is that the learning problem they are being asked to solve is poorly aligned in
time and weakly grounded in state.

### Are we failing to define intelligence properly?

The repo's definition is coherent: intelligence is grounded predictive
homeostatic control under capacity constraints. What may be missing is an
operational benchmark for that definition. If the goal is "learn the world,"
measure improvement within a lifetime, not only accumulated survival, food, and
exploration.

Useful metrics would include:

- food approach success after first sighting,
- hazard exit latency after first damage,
- food rate in the last quarter of a generation versus the first quarter,
- policy/noise ratio over a lifetime,
- recall valence separation for food-rich, barren, and danger states.

### Are they lacking survival instinct?

They have survival pressure, but not enough instinctive action scaffolding.
Biological survival instinct is not just "bad internal state hurts." It also
contains primitives such as withdraw, orient, persist, approach, freeze, and
escape. The current hardcoded klinotaxis turn-gain is one such primitive. More
of these primitives may be needed if the project expects fast emergence rather
than very long-timescale discovery.

## Recommended Experiments

Run these in order, because each isolates a different failure mode.

1. **Lag isolation**

   Set `vision_stride = 1` and `brain_tick_stride = 1` or `2`. Keep the world
   simple. If food chasing and danger avoidance improve quickly, the dominant
   issue is sensorimotor lag, not intelligence capacity.

2. **Hazard grounding**

   Emit `TOUCH_HAZARD` from `phase_vision_senses()` when the agent is in or
   near a danger biome. This preserves the "no symbolic labels in the brain"
   philosophy because the tag is still just a scalar feature, but it gives the
   learner a timely cue.

3. **Same-cycle interoceptive state**

   Feed current energy, integrity, and deltas into the feature vector directly
   from `physics_state`, not only through lagged packed sensory fields. The
   homeostasis pass already reads these fields safely.

4. **Value-shaped representation probe**

   Compare the Oja/PCA encoder against a value-shaped encoder or auxiliary
   predictive target that explicitly preserves features useful for forecasting
   homeostatic gradient. The point is not to hardcode food/danger, but to make
   the representation preserve survival-relevant differences.

5. **Within-lifetime learning metric**

   Add a score component for improvement over time: for example food rate or
   hazard-exit latency in the last quarter of a generation compared with the
   first. This makes "learning" selectable, not just "surviving."

6. **Exploration visibility test**

   Temporarily lower the exploration floor after stable positive/negative
   valence memories form. If learned pursuit appears but is masked by noise,
   the policy may be better than the viewport suggests.

---

## Bottom Line

The current system is not random and not empty of survival drive. It has a
valid homeostatic teacher, inherited learned state, and multiple adaptive
mechanisms. The reason food pursuit and danger avoidance remain inconsistent is
that the teacher is delayed and scalar, the sensory evidence is partly stale,
hazard cues are under-emitted, and the representation is not guaranteed to
preserve survival-relevant distinctions.

The next step should not be "make the agent more intelligent" in the abstract.
The next step should be to tighten the perception-action-homeostasis loop and
measure whether learning becomes visible when causal evidence is available.
